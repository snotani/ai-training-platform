<HowItWorks>What LLMs can and can&rsquo;t do</HowItWorks>

The big picture: LLMs are brilliant at working with language, and shaky whenever an answer has to be exactly, verifiably true.

The pattern: **language tasks** = use freely (and skim). **Exact-truth tasks** = verify, or give it the facts.

<HowItWorks>Why these limits exist</HowItWorks>

### 1. Hallucination

<Term>Hallucination</Term> happens because the model predicts plausible tokens. When it lacks a fact, it fills the gap with something that looks right - there&rsquo;s no built-in &ldquo;I checked this&rdquo; step. Highest risk: niche or recent topics, exact numbers, names, citations.

### 2. Bias

Trained on human-written internet text, so it absorbs human <Term>Bias</Term> and over-represents majority views. Ask it to &ldquo;describe a nurse and a CEO&rdquo; and, unprompted, it may default the nurse to &ldquo;she&rdquo; and the CEO to &ldquo;he.&rdquo;

### 3. Knowledge cutoff & no live data

It only &ldquo;knows&rdquo; up to its <Term>Knowledge cutoff</Term>, and can&rsquo;t see today&rsquo;s news or your private documents unless connected to tools/search (Level 8, RAG).

### 4. Weak at exact math & strict logic

It&rsquo;s matching language patterns, not running a calculator, so it can fumble arithmetic or &ldquo;count the letters&rdquo; tasks - unless it works step by step or uses tools.

### 5. Context limits

It can only hold so much text at once; very long inputs get truncated or key details get lost in the middle. Its working memory is the <Term k="context window">context window</Term>.

### 6. AI slop

Mass, low-effort generated content. Beyond being low value, <Term k="AI slop">AI slop</Term> pollutes the web - and future training data - creating a feedback loop.

<Callout type="warning" title="Confidence is not correctness">
The confidence needle can pin high while the answer is wrong. Read a confident tone as style, not proof.
</Callout>

<HowItWorks>How to mitigate</HowItWorks>

- **Verify** anything that matters against a trusted source (Lesson 2.6 teaches the technique).
- **Give it the facts** - paste the source or use retrieval - instead of trusting its memory.
- For logic/math, **ask for steps** or use a reasoning model/tool.
- Read confident tone as style, not proof.

<Expand>

- &ldquo;Calibration&rdquo; = whether a model&rsquo;s confidence matches its accuracy; LLMs are often over-confident.
- &ldquo;Sycophancy&rdquo;: models tend to agree with you - watch for it when you lead the question.
- Asking the model to critique its own answer can surface errors (a preview of self-evaluation in Level 6).

</Expand>
