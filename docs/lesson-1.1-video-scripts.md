# Lesson 1.1 — Short Video Scripts

Short-form video scripts (TikTok / Reels / Shorts, ≤60s each) based on **Lesson 1.1 — Types of AI**. Each script is hook-first and example-driven: open inside a relatable moment, teach through everyday examples, hold the jargon until an example earns it.

## Comment-to-DM CTA system

- **Keyword:** `TYPES` — viewers comment it and an auto-DM tool (e.g. ManyChat) sends the free course link. The comment volume also boosts the video.
- **Where used:** Videos 1 and 3 drive the course CTA. Video 2 instead uses an engagement question (guess-in-comments) to seed comments.
- **Tips:** say the keyword out loud *and* show it on screen; keep the CTA cue up for the full last ~5s; pin a comment with the keyword.

## Video 1 — "AI's been thinking for you for years"

**Angle:** AI's whole evolution is one everyday habit — finding an answer — slowly handing the *thinking* from you to the machine (encyclopedia → Google → ChatGPT). Ends with the course CTA.

> **AI didn't get smarter — you just stopped doing the thinking.**
>
> You haven't actually Googled anything in weeks. You just ask ChatGPT and move on.
>
> Rewind a bit. Back in the day, a question meant grabbing an encyclopedia or asking around — you did all the digging yourself.
>
> Then Google handed you the ten best links — but you still had to read them and figure out the answer.
>
> Now ChatGPT just gives you the answer, written out, done.
>
> Same question every time — but notice who's doing the work. Every step quietly handed more of the thinking from you to the machine. That's the whole story of AI.
>
> If that clicked, I made a free beginner course that explains all of it like this — comment TYPES and I'll DM you the link.

**Alt first lines (A/B):**
- "You've outsourced your brain in three quiet steps and never noticed."
- "In 20 years you went from doing all the thinking to almost none — here's how."

## Video 2 — "You used 3 different AIs today"

**Angle:** Lead with the everyday example, then name the type plainly — Netflix → machine learning, face filter → deep learning, ChatGPT → generative AI. Ends with a guess-in-comments engagement hook (Spotify is intentionally *not* one of the taught examples).

> **You used three completely different types of AI today — and you couldn't name a single one.**
>
> Netflix just told you you'll like this show with 92% certainty. It's not watching anything — it just looks at everything you've already watched and predicts the next one. That's machine learning.
>
> Slap a dog filter on your face and the ears stick to your head as you move — that one's reading the actual camera pixels to find your face. That's deep learning.
>
> Ask ChatGPT to reply to an awkward text — you give it one line, it writes the whole thing. That's generative AI, because it's making something brand new.
>
> Predict, recognize, create — three different jobs, and you hit all three before lunch.
>
> So tell me — Spotify's Discover Weekly, which one is that? Drop your answer in the comments.

**Alt first lines (A/B):**
- "Three different AIs ran your phone today and you thought they were all the same thing."
- "You already use all three types of AI — before you even have breakfast."

**Answer (for pinned reply, keep open in the video):** Spotify Discover Weekly = **Traditional Machine Learning** — it predicts songs from your listening-history table, same family as Netflix's picks.

## Video 3 — "Call the right AI"

**Angle:** Turn "which type?" into a fast game using the quick test — making something new → generative AI; messy input (photos/audio/text) → deep learning; tidy table → machine learning. Ends with the course CTA.

> **Most people grab the wrong AI for the job — let's see if you do.**
>
> Spot the broken product flying down a factory line — which AI do you grab? Two seconds. Go.
>
> Here's the test. Making something new? That's generative AI. Messy input like photos, audio, or text? Deep learning. Just a tidy table of numbers? Plain machine learning.
>
> So the broken product in photos — that's messy images — deep learning.
>
> Predict next quarter's sales from a spreadsheet? Tidy table — machine learning.
>
> Write a personal email to every single customer? You're making something new — generative AI.
>
> Making something, messy input, or a tidy table — that's the whole trick.
>
> Want the full version? I've got a free AI course — comment TYPES and I'll send it to your DMs.

**Alt first lines (A/B):**
- "Pick the wrong AI and you'll waste hours — here's how to never do that again."
- "I'll name a task, you call the right AI — bet you miss one."
